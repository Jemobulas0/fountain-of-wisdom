Base Attack Time: Not All Attack Speeds Are Equal

What This Guide Covers

Everyone knows that more attack speed makes you hit faster. Few know how Base Attack Time (BAT) factors into the equation - a hidden value that decides how much each point of attack speed is actually worth.

Two heroes with the exact same items can attack at very different rates. This guide explains why, and what you can do with that knowledge.

It's a long read, built in four parts:

  • Part I - The Mechanics. What BAT is, the formula behind every attack, the attack speed cap, and which number in your HUD actually tells you how fast you attack.
  • Part II - Practical Applications. How BAT changes the value of the items you buy, where the balance between damage and attack speed sits, and when an attack speed item is worth more than its numbers suggest.
  • Part III - Unique Hero Cases. Heroes with unusual BAT values, heroes who change their BAT mid-fight (Alchemist, Troll Warlord, Kez), and what happens when several BAT effects apply at once.
  • Part IV - Edge Cases. Attacks that ignore BAT completely, and what BAT does and doesn't change about your attack animation.

Short on time? Jump to the Summary at the end for the key takeaways, and the Quick Reference for every formula and table in one place.

Part I - The Mechanics
1. What BAT Is

Every unit in DotA has a Base Attack Time: the time between its attacks before any attack speed is added. That includes heroes, creeps, summons, Roshan, and even the Fountain.

Think of it as the unit's natural rhythm. A Melee Creep has a BAT of 1.0, so with no bonuses it attacks exactly once per second. Most heroes have a BAT of 1.7. If we don't take into account their agility and bonus attack speed, they will attack once every 1.7 seconds. Of course, in practice these things do exist, so think of BAT as a baseline the rest builds upon.

Two things to remember going forward:

  • Lower BAT = faster attacks. A hero with 1.4 BAT attacks faster than a hero with 1.7 BAT when their attack speeds are the same.
  • Most heroes share the same BAT, but not all. Hero BATs currently range from 1.4 (Juggernaut, Anti-Mage) to 2.0 (Hoodwink), with 1.7 being considered the default one, since it's what most heroes have. A few heroes can even change their BAT during a fight. Both are covered in Part III.

Attack speed changes how fast you move through your rhythm. BAT is the rhythm itself. The next section shows exactly how the two combine.

2. The Formula

Every attack in DotA comes from one simple calculation. First, the game adds up your total attack speed:

Total Attack Speed = Base Attack Speed + Agility + Bonus Attack Speed
  • Base Attack Speed is 100 for most heroes, though some start a bit lower or higher.
  • Agility adds 1 attack speed per point.
  • Bonus Attack Speed comes from items, abilities, and talents.

A few effects also change your attack speed by a percentage. They are applied after everything above is added up.

Then the game uses your total attack speed and your BAT to decide how often you attack:

Attacks per Second = Total Attack Speed ÷ (100 × BAT)
Time Between Attacks = (100 × BAT) ÷ Total Attack Speed

Example. A level 1 hero with 1.7 BAT, 100 base attack speed and 20 agility:

  • Total attack speed: 100 + 20 = 120
  • Attacks per second: 120 ÷ (100 × 1.7) = 120 ÷ 170 = 0.71
  • Time between attacks: 170 ÷ 120 = 1.42 seconds

Now give the exact same hero a BAT of 1.4 instead:

  • Total attack speed: still 120
  • Attacks per second: 120 ÷ 140 = 0.86
  • Time between attacks: 140 ÷ 120 = 1.17 seconds

Same stats, same items, and that hero now attacks about 21% faster. That difference comes entirely from BAT.

One more thing to notice: attack speed and time between attacks are inversely related. Double your attack speed and you halve the time between attacks. Triple it and the time drops to a third.

3. Attack Speed vs. BAT

Attack speed and BAT do different jobs. Attack speed is what you gain over the game, through agility, items, abilities, and talents. BAT is a fixed property of your hero. It decides how much each point of that attack speed is worth.

Look at the formula again: attacks per second = attack speed ÷ (100 × BAT). BAT sits on the bottom of that division. So it doesn't add or subtract from your attack speed. It scales every single point of it.

What 100 attack speed is worth at different BATs:

BAT+100 attack speed gives you
1.4+0.71 attacks per second
1.7+0.59 attacks per second
1.9+0.53 attacks per second

The same 100 attack speed gets a 1.4 BAT hero about 21% more attacks than it gets a 1.7 BAT hero. That ratio never changes, no matter how much attack speed you have.

The gap grows as the game goes on. The ratio stays fixed, but the real difference in attacks keeps growing. Compare a 1.4 BAT hero and a 1.7 BAT hero at the same attack speed:

Attack speed1.4 BAT1.7 BATDifference
120 (early game)0.86 /s0.71 /s0.15 /s
400 (mid-late game)2.86 /s2.35 /s0.50 /s
700 (maximum)5.00 /s4.12 /s0.88 /s

Early game, the difference is barely noticeable. It's more obvious when you have heroes like Juggernaut vs Treant Protector facing each other in lane. By the late game, the lower-BAT hero gets almost one extra attack every second.

Converting between BATs. To find out how much attack speed one hero needs to match another:

Attack Speed Needed = Their Attack Speed × (Your BAT ÷ Their BAT)

For example, a 1.7 BAT hero with 300 attack speed attacks exactly as fast as a 1.4 BAT hero with only 247 (300 × 1.4 ÷ 1.7). In other words, the 1.4 BAT hero gets the same result from 53 fewer points of attack speed.

With that said, itemization in DotA is an extremely interesting topic. So buying the highest attack speed possible at the cost of other, more important stats and effects for your particular hero is rarely the best choice. More on that in Part II.

4. The Limits

Attack speed has a minimum and a maximum value. The game clamps your total attack speed between them, after every bonus and every slow has been applied.

The maximum: 700

Almost no hero can go above 700 total attack speed, no matter how many items or buffs they stack. The cap is the same for every hero, regardless of their base attack speed. Anything beyond 700 is simply wasted.

Because the cap is fixed at 700 attack speed, BAT decides how many attacks per second that cap actually means for each hero. A 1.4 BAT hero at the cap attacks 5 times per second. A 1.9 BAT hero at the same cap attacks only 3.68 times. Section 7 covers this in detail.

The exception: Marci

Currently, Marci's Unleash is the only ability to break the cap. It grants 700/925/1150 bonus attack speed and removes the attack speed limit entirely while active. The trade-off is that between her Fury combos she can't attack at all for 1.5 seconds.

The minimum: 20 (usually)

No matter how heavily you're slowed, your attack speed won't drop below a floor. For most heroes that floor is 20, which at 1.7 BAT means one attack every 8.5 seconds.

The floor moves with a hero's base attack speed. The minimum is always 80 below the hero's base attack speed:

  • Base attack speed 100 → minimum 20
  • Base attack speed 110 (e.g. Sven) → minimum 30
  • Base attack speed 85 (Tiny) → minimum 5, or one attack every 34 seconds

So a hero with a lower base attack speed can be slowed further than average, while a hero with a higher one can't be slowed as hard.

Non-hero units don't follow this rule. Roshan has 200 base attack speed, yet he can still be slowed down to 20, the same as any other unit. At his 2.0 BAT, that's one attack every 10 seconds.

5. What Your HUD Is Actually Telling You

Hover over your hero's attack speed in the HUD and you'll see two numbers: your attack speed, and the time between your attacks in brackets. They answer two different questions.

The attack speed number shows how close you are to the cap. It's your real total attack speed, and the cap is 700 for every hero. When it reads 700, more attack speed does nothing.

The bracket shows how fast you actually attack. The attack speed number doesn't include BAT. The time between attacks does, because it's the result of the full formula.

That's why the attack speed number can't tell you which hero attacks faster. Take Juggernaut and Sven, both at the cap:

Attack speedTime between attacks
Juggernaut (1.4 BAT)7000.20 seconds
Sven (1.9 BAT)7000.27 seconds

Both show the same attack speed, but Juggernaut attacks 5 times per second and Sven only about 3.7 times.

The rule of thumb: use the attack speed number to know when to stop buying attack speed. Use the bracket to compare how fast heroes actually attack.

Part II - Practical Applications
6. Same Item, Different Value

Buy the same item on two heroes with different BAT and you'll get two different results.

Your damage per second comes down to two things: how hard you hit, and how often you hit.

DPS = Damage per Hit × Attacks per Second

BAT controls the "how often" part. So a lower BAT doesn't only make attack speed items stronger. It makes every item that adds to your right-click damage stronger, damage items included.

Example. Two heroes, each with 200 attack speed and 100 damage per hit. One has 1.4 BAT, the other 1.9 BAT.

1.4 BAT1.9 BAT
Starting DPS143105
Buy +100 attack speed214 (+71)158 (+53)
Or buy +50 damage instead214 (+71)158 (+53)

The 1.4 BAT hero gains about 36% more from both items. Attack speed items aren't special here. The low BAT hero simply gets more out of everything that feeds their right-click.

The same holds against armor. Armor reduces each hit by a percentage, so it cuts both heroes' damage by the same proportion and the comparison doesn't change.

This answers half of the question from Section 3. Low BAT makes your damage items better, but it doesn't tell you which item to buy. Whether your next item should be damage or attack speed depends on what you already have. That's Section 8.

The example above is, of course, simplified for clarity's sake. As mentioned before, in a real game you have to take many more factors into account when itemizing your hero. But for now, let's get back to the topic at hand.

7. BAT Sets Your Ceiling

Section 4 showed that attack speed is capped at 700. What that cap means depends on the hero's BAT, because BAT decides how many attacks per second 700 attack speed actually gives you.

Maximum Attacks per Second = 7 ÷ BAT

Two heroes can both max out their attack speed and still have completely different ceilings. A 1.4 BAT hero at the cap attacks about 36% more often than a 1.9 BAT hero at the cap, and nothing either of them buys can change that.

What this means once you're capped. At 700 attack speed, attack speed items stop doing anything. From that point, your attack count can only go up one way: by lowering your BAT. That's why BAT-changing abilities are at their strongest in the late game. They're the only way past a ceiling that items can't break.

Example: Troll Warlord. Battle Trance gives up to 200 attack speed, and a full stack of Fervor adds up to 300 more. With a few items on top, Troll can reach the cap during a fight. In ranged form, the cap gives him 4.12 attacks per second. Switch to melee, where his BAT drops to 1.4, and it becomes 5.00. That's about 21% more attacks, at the exact same attack speed.

8. What This Means for Itemization

Section 6 showed that low BAT makes every right-click item better. It didn't answer the real question: should your next item be damage or attack speed? The math answers that, and BAT plays no part in the answer.

Picture your DPS as a rectangle

Your damage per hit is the width. Your attack speed is the height. The area is your DPS. In all three examples, damage and attack speed add up to the same 454 points. The only difference is how they're split between the two stats.

  • Buying damage makes the rectangle wider. How much area you add depends on how tall it already is.
  • Buying attack speed makes it taller. How much area you add depends on how wide it already is.

Each stat is worth more the more you have of the other one. That's why stacking only one of them stops paying off.

The balance point

If you have a fixed number of points to split between damage and attack speed, your DPS is highest when the two numbers are equal, the same way a square gets the most area out of a fixed perimeter.

Take Juggernaut (1.4 BAT) with 454 total points, split two different ways:

Damage + Attack speedDPS
253 + 201363.2
193 + 261359.8

The more balanced split wins. But notice the difference is only about 1%. Near the balance point, being a little off barely matters. The further you drift, the faster you lose:

Split (454 total)DPS compared to a perfect balance
227 + 2270%
253 + 201-1.3%
193 + 261-2.2%
354 + 100-31%

The lesson isn't to chase exact balance. It's to avoid being heavily lopsided.

Gold changes where the balance sits

In a real game, damage and attack speed don't cost the same gold per point. The true balance point is:

Your attack speed ÷ your damage = gold cost per damage point ÷ gold cost per attack speed point

If a point of damage costs twice as much as a point of attack speed, your ideal build has about twice as much attack speed as damage. The shape stays the same, though: flat near the balance point, steep at the extremes.

Comparing two items

To compare a damage item with an attack speed item directly:

Damage the item gives × your current attack speed
vs.
Attack speed the item gives × your current damage per hit

Whichever number is bigger adds more DPS. These are the two strips of the rectangle. Divide each by the item's cost to compare DPS per gold.

Example: a hero with 400 attack speed and 120 damage, choosing between +50 damage and +100 attack speed:

  • Damage item: 50 × 400 = 20,000
  • Attack speed item: 100 × 120 = 12,000

The damage item wins, even though the attack speed item's number looks twice as big.

When the balance shifts

  • Flat on-hit effects. Procs that add a fixed amount of damage per attack, like chain lightning or bash damage, grow with every extra attack but don't grow with damage items. They tip the balance toward attack speed. Effects that apply something on hit, like slows, work the same way. Many of these effects trigger by chance, using Pseudo-Random Distribution. Read more about it in our Pseudo-Random Distribution Guide.
  • Flat damage block. Some targets block a fixed amount of damage from every hit. More hits means more damage lost to block, so it tips the balance toward damage.
  • The attack speed cap. Near 700, extra attack speed is partly wasted. At 700, it's worth nothing. Damage is the only way up.
  • Crit and armor don't shift it. Both scale every hit by the same percentage, so they affect damage and attack speed items equally.

In practice, DotA itemization is rarely about raw stats. Most of the time you buy an item for what it does: BKB for Debuff Immunity, Blink for initiation, and so on. Those decisions come first. The math in this section is for the moments when you do have a real choice between damage and attack speed. Knowing it well enough to make that call mid-game is what separates a good build from the best one.

10. Heroes Who Set Their BAT

A few heroes can replace their BAT with a different value during the game. Their attack speed doesn't change, but every point of it suddenly becomes more valuable.

Alchemist - Chemical Rage

11.2+41.7%

At level 3, Alchemist attacks 70% more often while his ultimate is active, with the exact same items. His level 20 talent lowers it by another 0.1, down to 0.9, one of the lowest BATs any hero can reach. How the talent combines with the ultimate is covered in Section 12.

Troll Warlord - Battle Stance

Troll's ranged form uses the default 1.7 BAT. Switching to melee lowers it to 1.4, the same as Juggernaut and Anti-Mage, which means about 21% more attacks. He also gains 1 armor for every 30 bonus attack speed, so in melee form every attack speed item feeds both his damage and his survivability.

Kez - Switch Discipline

Kez is the most interesting case, because neither weapon is strictly better:

In Sai, Kez attacks about 27% more often than in Katana. The Katana trades that speed for reach and its own bonuses, including extra damage on the first hit after switching to it. Kez's BAT is a choice he makes constantly, not a fixed upgrade.

11. Heroes Who Reduce Their BAT

Instead of replacing their BAT with a new value, some effects lower it by a certain amount. There are two kinds: flat reductions, which subtract a fixed number of seconds, and percentage reductions, which cut BAT by a proportion.

Broodmother - Insatiable Hunger (level 25 talent)

While Insatiable Hunger is active, Broodmother's BAT drops by 0.15, from 1.7 to 1.55. That's about 10% more attacks. Since patch 7.41, the talent also affects her Spiderlings within 800 range, so the whole brood attacks faster during her ultimate.

Lycan - Summon Wolves (level 25 talent)

Lycan's wolves already have a low BAT that improves as the ability levels up:

His level 25 talent reduces their BAT by 25%. At level 4, that takes the wolves from 0.9 down to 0.675, which means about 33% more attacks for every wolf.

Flat vs. percentage

A flat reduction is worth more the lower your BAT already is. Broodmother's -0.15 is a 9% cut from 1.7, but the same -0.15 would be a 15% cut from 1.0. A percentage reduction always gives the same boost: -25% BAT means 33% more attacks, whatever the starting value.

12. When Several BAT Effects Apply at Once

Reductions apply on top of set effects. A reduction always subtracts from your current BAT, after any set effect has done its job. The order doesn't matter: whether the reduction or the set effect comes first, the result is the same.

Example: Alchemist. His level 20 talent is a reduction (-0.1). Chemical Rage is a set effect (1.0 at level 3). Chemical Rage replaces his BAT with 1.0, the talent then subtracts 0.1, and he ends up at 0.9. If reductions applied to his base 1.7 before Chemical Rage overwrote it, the talent would do nothing during his ultimate, which is exactly when it matters.

Two more rules

Currently, no hero can actually have these combinations, but they're worth knowing in case future patches make them possible:

  • Set effects don't combine. If two effects both try to set your BAT, only one applies. Normally that's the first one applied, even if the second would give a lower BAT.
  • Reductions add up. If a hero had two reductions active at once, both would apply.

Neutral creeps

Every neutral creep has a 2.0 BAT, but they make up for it with a high base attack speed, usually between 120 and 200 instead of 100. A neutral with 150 attack speed and 2.0 BAT attacks at the same rate as a hero with about 128 attack speed and 1.7 BAT.

Part IV - Edge Cases
14. Attacks That Ignore BAT

Not every attack in DotA follows the BAT formula. Some abilities perform attacks on their own fixed timer, completely independent of the hero's BAT and attack speed.

Examples include Ember Spirit's Sleight of Fist, which attacks every enemy in its area one after another on a fixed interval, and Monkey King's Wukong's Command, whose soldiers attack on a fixed interval of their own. Legion Commander's Moment of Courage counter-attack also fires outside her normal attack rhythm.

These attacks follow their own rules, so BAT and attack speed don't affect them. Because of that, they're outside the scope of this guide.

15. BAT and Attack Animation

Every attack has two parts: the foreswing, the wind-up before the hit lands, and the backswing, the follow-through after it. Attack speed makes both of them faster.

BAT doesn't. It only controls how long you wait between swings. When your BAT drops, the swing itself stays exactly as fast as before. What shrinks is the idle time between one swing and the next.

This matters for anything that depends on the wind-up, like last-hitting and canceling your attack. A lower BAT makes you attack more often, but it doesn't make each individual hit land any sooner after you start swinging. Only attack speed does that.

Closing
16. A Short History of BAT

Back in the original DotA, none of this was public. There were no published formulas, no wikis with exact numbers. To understand how attack speed really worked, I had to test it myself: timing attacks, changing one variable at a time, and working backwards to the math. BAT was one of the things those tests revealed.

BAT has been part of DotA since the original map, and Valve has used it as a balancing tool ever since.

  • Anti-Mage once had a BAT as low as 1.35. It was raised back to 1.45 in an early DotA 2 patch, and today he sits at 1.4.
  • Alchemist's Chemical Rage has been retuned many times. It started at 1.45 / 1.35 / 1.2, was gradually lowered over several patches, and today it's 1.2 / 1.1 / 1.0.
  • Spirit Bear used to have a BAT that improved with each level of the ability, from 1.75 down to 1.45. Today he has a flat 1.5.
  • Lycan's Shapeshift used to lower his BAT. It no longer does.
  • The attack speed cap has been raised twice: from 500 to 600, and then to 700 in patch 7.20.
  • Base attack speed was 100 for every hero until patch 7.22, when Valve started giving heroes different values. That's what created the different attack speed floors from Section 4.
17. Summary
  • BAT is the base time between your attacks. Lower BAT means faster attacks. Most heroes have 1.7. Your attacks per second = attack speed ÷ (100 × BAT).
  • Attack speed is capped at 700 for everyone (Marci being the only exception), but BAT decides what that cap is worth: 5 attacks per second at 1.4 BAT, only 3.68 at 1.9.
  • Understand your HUD better. The attack speed number shows how close you are to the cap. The time between attacks in brackets shows how fast you actually attack.
  • Low BAT makes every right-click item better, damage and attack speed alike. Which one to buy depends on balance: avoid being heavily lopsided. On-hit effects favor attack speed; damage block and the cap favor damage.
  • Some heroes can alter their BAT. A set effect replaces your BAT; a reduction subtracts from whatever it currently is.
